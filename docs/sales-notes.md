## 2026-08-12 — Thiel: sales/distribution as monopoly power

Yes — this is a very important Peter Thiel idea, and the first screenshot captures the interesting part:

Superior sales + distribution can create monopoly-like power even without a strongly differentiated product.

The implication is that founders often obsess over “Is my product technically better?” when the more decisive question can be “Can I reach and acquire the market much better than everyone else?”

For a startup, think of the moat as roughly:

Product × Distribution × Retention

A technically excellent product with almost no distribution can lose to a merely good product with an exceptional distribution engine.

And there’s an important nuance: the caption underneath actually shifts toward Thiel’s other argument—escaping competition through product differentiation. Those aren’t contradictory. The strongest company ideally has both: something meaningfully different and a distribution channel competitors struggle to reproduce.

For something like aft.page, this would mean the strategic question isn’t only “How do I make deployment better than Vercel/Netlify/Cloudflare?” It is also “What distribution channel could I own where they aren’t naturally positioned?” Agent ecosystems, MCPs, coding-agent integrations, generated-app workflows, or a viral “deploy/share” loop could potentially matter more than another 20 infrastructure features.
