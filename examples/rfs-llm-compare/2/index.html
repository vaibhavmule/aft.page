What changed vs round 1

Round 1 was four greenfield kits. Round 2 is four operating plans for a company that already has Drop, CLI, MCP, claim, invite, vault, rollback. Nobody told you to build aft deploy. The fight is: public-first, freeze vs safety, who you sell, how you charge, and whether a static page counts as Small Software.

Gemini

“The permission and identity layer for agent-generated apps.”

Sells control, not hosting. Cloudflare executes; AFT is the governance boundary between agent code and company data. Largest beachhead of the four.

  • ICP: eng managers at 50–500. Sarah, analytics lead at a 120-person fintech, gave up hosting a refund checker and Slack’d an HTML file.
  • Not first: indie public apps — Vercel/CF already win.
  • Price: $15/builder/month, viewers free. Defends it as the way to not tax tiny audiences.
  • Metric: Weekly Active Consumers (people who open an app they did not create).
  • Post-gate: egress-stripping proxy, auto domain wall (invite alex@company.com implies team), invitee capability prompt, fork-to-inspect, plugin installer, audit, domain directory.
  • This month: keep freeze, but hand-onboard 10 eng managers. Private-by-default. No SAML — email-domain isolation. No native DB/queues.

Grok

“aft.page makes agent-built small software live, claimable, and shareable like a Google Doc.”

Most faithful to what you already wrote. Least invention. Treats the freeze as law, not a vibe.

  • ICP: 2–8 person teams already on agents. Senior engineer at a 12-person SaaS who invited two finance people to an expense tool — that is the proof gate.
  • Not first: anyone who needs Workspace/Entra/SCIM/app store.
  • Price: pay when a claimed project is private with >1 member, or when secrets/capabilities are used. Not per seat, not per deploy.
  • Metric: claimed + accepted invite that returns in 7 days; second-person sessions.
  • Post-gate: company env inherited by every deploy, capabilities that actually gate, isolation for worker/Next invitees, org discovery, thin audit, tiny persistent data, untrusted-by-default mode.
  • This month: close stranger → claim → invite → return. Treat non-static as “do not invite yet” until isolation is real. Death = URL is free everywhere and nobody bothers to claim.

ChatGPT

“AFT is the permissioned cloud where agent-made small software gets a durable URL and shares like a Google Doc.”

Most precise on trust architecture. One freeze exception: unclaimed apps must not be public URLs.

  • ICP: eng managers at 20–100. Friday release-readiness dashboard that currently dies as Slack screenshots.
  • Not first: nontechnical teams in regulated enterprises — you cannot invite them safely yet.
  • Price: one org control-plane subscription, unlimited apps/viewers, meter execution/storage/egress.
  • Metric: weekly co-used apps (invite accepted + two people, separate days).
  • Do not build (12 mo): AI builder, Firebase-like BaaS, fork/marketplace/app store.
  • After proof, in order: split control-plane cookies from user code → sandbox isolation → capability broker deny-by-default → recipient trust screen → company env profiles → domain OIDC (not SCIM) → audit/provenance.
  • This week: 40 messages to eng managers who already have a localhost tool and can name the second user. Token-gate unclaimed now; nothing else before Sep 10.

Claude

“Agent-built software, shared like a Google Doc.” Lead with claim → invite → revoke, not deploy.

Sharpest operating plan. The invite is the product. Sell to Alex; design for her cofounder. Most willing to pick defaults.

  • ICP: “agent person” at 5–50. Alex, ops lead at a 14-person logistics shop. Not the nontechnical creator yet — they arrive as invitees. Never-first: IT buying an internal app platform.
  • Price: $49/month workspace when a second app appears on the same email domain. Unlimited apps, ≤25 members, viewers free forever. Individuals free forever.
  • Metric: second-person week-2 return. A thousand deploys with no accepted invites is a failing company.
  • Post-gate: private-by-default for signed-in deploys; viewer consent card; wrap CF sandbox + finish egress stub; one KV/SQLite per app; email-domain workspace as the SKU; audit; ownership transfer; worker GA for strangers; bury next.
  • Do not build: SSO/SCIM, any generation surface, fork/remix/public gallery.
  • This week: instrument the full invite funnel daily; walk the invitee path (email, mobile magic link); DM agent users and ask “who’s the second person?” If none, they are not ICP.

What all four are saying

  • Stop selling deploy. Agent → URL is Drop / Vercel / ChatGPT Sites / every plugin. The company is what happens after claim.
  • Moat is identity, permissions, secrets, capabilities, revocation, audit — not compute. Wrap Cloudflare.
  • Do not become a builder. Neutrality across agents is the distribution (MCP). Generation makes Cursor/Anthropic competitors.
  • North star is a second person who returns, not deployment count. Solo deploys are a failure mode.
  • Do not tax viewers or apps. Charge builders, workspace, or governance + usage.
  • This month is hand-to-hand: find people with an existing localhost tool and a named coworker. Freeze is only legitimate if it forces that loop.
  • Public unclaimed URLs are a trust hole. Three of four want it fixed now; Grok treats it as a later default decision.
  • No SAML/SCIM this year. Domain + email invites cover 1–8 users. Nontechnical people need a consent/safety guarantee, not a settings page.
  • Death: Vercel/CF/chat vendors add “share with email”; or an unsafe-code scandal; or 10,000 MCP deploys and zero second users.

  • Org subscription + metered resources, not a seat or $49 flat.

Claude only

  • Pete’s objection: without state, Small Software is a brochure. One KV, one MCP tool, no SDK.
  • $49 workspace when a second same-domain app appears. Charge that, this year.
  • Bury next from docs; static + worker through mid-2027.
  • 30-day idle delete as a feature (“compost”). Ownership transfer when people leave.
  • Design effort → invitee, not deployer DX. Demo ends on the second person’s screen.
