# Vaibhav. Internal voice; publish when you mean it.

In 2011 someone asked me: can you deploy WordPress? How long?

I said five minutes. I did it.

AWS login. Bitnami WordPress. Public URI. Admin. Get started.

That was the whole product: a clock, a URL, a human in. Nobody wanted EC2
explained. They wanted the thing live.

2011 small software was WordPress.
2026 small software is whatever an agent just made.

The app changed. The job did not. Time-to-URL. See time-to-url.txt.
Look at it every day on ops.aft.page.

As of today, deployment is still friction. People cannot deploy the simple
thing. I still remember that five-minute day, and I still measure us against it.
Five minutes is no longer a flex. The bar is: agent hits deploy, you open a URL
before you look away.

I can already do this for myself. I deploy a web app and it is easy. AFT exists
so a one-person team — or two, or three, like me — gets that same feeling
without becoming an AWS person. Cloudflare already sold us the primitives. We
wrap them. We do not rebuild the cloud. We delete the ceremony.

We do not have socials. No Twitter, no Instagram, no LinkedIn theatre. External
surface is email. That is fine. Distribution is not a brand account. Highest
leverage is a plugin: install in Cursor (then every other agent that speaks the
same format) → Deploy with AFT → live URL. After the plugin exists, submit the
MCP where people actually look for MCP servers. Then someone can try without
asking me.

Honesty, 9 Aug 2026: nobody has deployed. Forget deploy twice. Pay is secondary.
Pay comes later — when they keep the app, share it, or hook it to their data.
This week is not a price. This week is one stranger who tries.

The company is a cloud for small software. Agent made it → aft makes it live.
If I cannot give someone the 2011 feeling for the thing their agent just built,
I am not a CEO. I am a guy with Workers.
