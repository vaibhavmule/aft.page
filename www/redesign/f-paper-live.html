What aft does

Three things, done properly.

Not a control plane. Not an infrastructure suite. Just the shape of putting software online and handing it to people.

  • (1)

    Deploy ● ships today

    Drop a folder, let your agent call MCP, or paste a link. Static files and SPAs become a live HTTPS URL in seconds.

    interface: drop · MCP · CLI · cURL
    limits: 500 files · 25 MB each
  • (2)

    Share

    Public when you want, private when it's internal. Invite people by email like a shared Doc — and revoke them just as easily, no redeploy.

    access: invite · view · edit · revoke
  • (3)

    Keep

    Your URL stays yours across every update. Roll back the moment something's wrong. When the project's over, take it down — no orphaned bills.

    lifecycle: ship · update · rollback · stop

FAQ

The questions people actually ask.

Is it really free right now?

Yes. Going live is free during beta, and claiming your URL to keep it is free too. Private apps, invite-by-email sharing, and per-site secrets are part of Personal — $19/mo after 30 days free. We will email the waitlist before charging anyone.

What can I put on aft?

Single HTML documents, or multi-file static sites and SPAs (include an index.html). Limits: 500 files, 25 MB per file, 100 MB total. If your software needs a server that runs code, that's the roadmap — not today.

Why not Vercel, Netlify, or Cloudflare Pages?

Those are right when you already have a git repo, CI, preview PRs, or a production team. If you have agent output or a dist/ folder and want a live HTTPS URL in seconds with no account, that's the job aft exists for.

Who owns the link?

Anyone can deploy anonymously and get a link. Claim it and it's yours: same URL on every update, rollback, private access, invite by email. Unclaimed sites are deleted after 30 days idle.
