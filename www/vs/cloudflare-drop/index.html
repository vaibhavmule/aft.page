When Cloudflare Drop is the right call

  • A one-off static file or folder you just need a link for
  • A throwaway preview you don't plan to keep
  • No need for identity, private sharing, or updates in place

When aft.page is the right call

  • You want the URL to persist and be owned, not expire
  • Share privately with specific people and revoke later
  • Update the page in place at the same URL
  • Hold secrets or add dynamic /api/* endpoints
  • Let your AI agent deploy directly over MCP
  • Attach your own domain after claim

aft.page is built on Cloudflare — it doesn't rebuild those primitives. It adds the small-software lifecycle raw static hosting leaves to you: ownership, private sharing, redeploy, and secrets.

Common questions

How is aft.page different from Cloudflare Drop?

Cloudflare Drop is great for a fast, throwaway static upload to a temporary URL. aft.page adds the lifecycle and permission layer on top: a durable URL you can claim and keep, private sharing by invite, redeploy in place at the same URL, secrets, custom domains, and native agent deploys over MCP.

Does aft.page run on Cloudflare?

Yes. aft.page is built on Cloudflare. It does not replace Cloudflare's primitives — it adds the small-software lifecycle (ownership, sharing, secrets, redeploy) that raw static hosting leaves to you.

Can I keep and update a page instead of it expiring?

Yes. Claim a deploy to keep the URL, then redeploy in place to update it without changing the link, and make it private or invite people when you need to.
